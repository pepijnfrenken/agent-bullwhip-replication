# WAVE7.md — the decision-native lane: closed-choice readout, outcome-trained, null on training

mechanism behind the failure, and what is worth doing next. Pre-registered design:
`WAVE7_SPEC.md` (approved 2026-10-05) + `../audits/WAVE7-AUDIT-1.md` / `../audits/WAVE7-AUDIT-2.md` amendments.

recomputed from the raw artifacts in this checkout, not copied; where the two disagree the raw
jsonl wins (no disagreement found — see §9 check note in §1).

**Verdict up front: the training stage is a null-to-negative result.** C1 fails on every trained
arm — all six are *worse* than the untrained readout, several at 0/30 wins. The pre-registered
permutation gate fails at 0.000 invariance on all six trained arms. The one real positive is the
interface: the closed-choice readout removes the free-form rogue tail that dominated the wave-6
evals (rogue orders 0 across all 21 wave-7 protocol arms (427 run records, 61,488 decisions) vs 7–188 per wave-6 arm), and the *untrained* readout
lands in the C2 "strong" tier (10,464 ≤ 12.8k) on the single step-argmax game.

## 2. Method recap

- Readout: one forward pass over Qwen3-0.6B (pinned snapshot `c1899de2`); options rendered as
  single-token letters **A..U → orders 0..40 step 2**; softmax over the 21 label logits; argmax
  primary, sampled (seeded 1000+i) as the wave-6 T=0.7 counterpart.
- Arms: `untrained` (the C1 control) and 3 seeds × 2 stages — `sft` (soft-target CE on simulator
  outcome labels) and `rlcd` (exact all-21 expected-cost PG from the SFT checkpoint). LoRA r=8/α=16
  on attention projections, base frozen.
- Labels: E[J] over M=16 CRN futures per (state, candidate); continuation = order-up-to floor
  **fixed (θ=3.5, λ=0.2)**, tuned out-of-sample (train 8,766 / validation 9,066 vs untuned
  15,544/15,997); τ = 0.5·std_a E[J]; t ≥ 34 masked; 95,200 states, seed 6000. Round 2 (AUDIT-1
  fix 7): relabel on 5,760 current-policy states per seed before RLCD.
- Eval: **step-argmax n=1** (deterministic; labeled single cost), **step-sampled n=30** and
  **noisy-argmax n=30** (seeds 1000+i); inferential gates on noisy n=30 (AUDIT-1 fix 5).
- Floors (30 passes, `../../results/wave7_floors.json`): step wf_tuned 3,206 (also the n=1 game),
  untuned 3,681, oracle 3,206; noisy wf 5,346, untuned 5,423, oracle 4,594.

### Interpretation

The pick is neither value-preserving nor letter-preserving in general — reshuffling the grid moves
it to a nearby *letter* on the RLCD arms (mean |Δletter| ≤ 0.64, mean |Δvalue| 11–22) and to a
scattered set on SFT and untrained (|Δletter| 1.5–9.4). Nothing in any arm reads the option values
the way the gate demanded.

The RLCD arms are effectively **single-letter policies**: seed0 picks letter R in 512/512 states
(order 34 on the canonical grid), seed1 letter P (494–511/512, order 30), seed2 letter N
(474–512/512, order 26). Their protocol runs are near-constant — every role in every noisy game
emits one order value for all 36 weeks (28/30 runs exactly constant; seed1 runs 24 and 26 emit {30, 32}) — at exactly those bad values, which is why their costs sit
at 27.7k–37.5k with CV 0.01. The letter each RLCD run locks onto is the letter SFT had sharpened
(R = 34), or two to four grid steps off it (P, N) after RLCD's mode-seeking PG pushed the already-dominant
letter over the edge. That is collapse, not control: with λ=0.2 demand averaging ~8–10 units/week,
a constant order of 26–34 units per role per week accumulates inventory faster than the game can
absorb, and the cost floor for that regime is exactly the ~28k–37k observed.

SFT ended in between (4–6 distinct orders/role, modal order 34 at share 0.56–0.83) but at the same
wrong modal value: the label set itself pushed the argmax letter toward R. The round-2 relabel
targets are bimodal at the grid edges — target argmax at order 40 in 35–52% of states and at
order 0 in 27.5–41.7% (`policy_labels_seed{0,1,2}.jsonl`; consistent with the labeler's measured
edge-argmin rate 0.71 and AUDIT-1's grid-truncation warning, risk 5) — and SFT's 120 steps moved
train top-1 agreement only 0.03 (seed 2: 0.00) → 0.13–0.25 while dragging the full-vocab letter mass from 0.997
to 0.963–0.973 (the A1-predicted erosion, recorded in `train_log.jsonl`). The net was enough to
shift the letter prior from E(8) to R(34) — a *worse* prior, since the game's mean demand is 8 —
without installing state-conditioning. Hence C1's sign: trained < untrained everywhere.

The untrained control behaves exactly as AUDIT-1 §3b predicted for a letter prior: letter E
(order 8) in 474/512 states, invariance 0.000 under shuffles. Its accidental virtue is that E's
value (8) sits near the demand mean — that, plus ~3 distinct orders per role of state-driven
wobble, is what buys 11.4k–11.6k medians and the C2 strong tier. This is luck of the letter, not
competence, and should not be read as a working policy.

## 6. What is null vs what is positive

**Null / negative:**
- Training the readout (SFT and RLCD, 3 seeds each) made the policy worse: C1 fails 6/6, several
  arms at 0/30 wins. The training stage moved the letter prior toward worse order values (E=8 →
  R=34 dominant) without buying state-conditioned control.
- Value-invariance under option permutation: 0.000 on every arm. The interface did not make the
  model read values; training did not either.
- C4 on the binding variant: seeds disagree for both stages (RLCD because each seed collapsed to a
  different constant letter).
- R1: RLCD loses to SFT on the binding arm; not adopted.

**Positive / real:**
- **The closed-choice format kills the rogue tail.** 0 rogue orders (≥1e5) and parse_fail=0 across
  all 427 run records across the 21 arms, vs wave-6 free-form arms with 58–188 rogue orders and
  34–40% parse failures. Every C3 pairing wins raw, including untrained.
- The **untrained** readout is a stable, state-slightly-varied policy at 11.4k–11.6k medians —
  C2 strong tier on the single step game (10,464). Demonstrably letter-luck (§4), but it shows the
  decision-native interface gives a *usable, reproducible control* arm out of the box, which the
  free-form lane never had.
- The pipeline: labeler → SFT → collect → relabel → RLCD ran clean end-to-end on 3 seeds after
  the AUDIT-2 fix bundle (job 390955, all exits 0), and the eval stage is fully automated
  (398422, failed=0). The infrastructure for the next attempt exists and is cheap.

## 7. What this implies + next steps

Implication: at 0.6B with LoRA-on-attention, outcome-label training of a letter readout optimizes
*which letter*, not *what the letter means*. The labeler's edge-heavy targets (0.71 edge-argmin)
plus a 21-way softmax over positions is a recipe for prior-shift toward one hot letter — exactly
what was measured. The interface hypothesis (format fixes parse/rogue) is confirmed; the
value-sensitivity hypothesis (training puts it in) is falsified at this scale and this label set.
Three options, roughly costed:

1. **Readout-side fix — value-conditioned training targets (recommended).** Make the model's input
   carry the grid identity so letter and value cannot be conflated: permuted-option augmentation
   during SFT (pre-registered in AUDIT-1 amendment 1 but not implemented), or drop letters
   entirely and regress/softmax on the *rendered numeric* option lines with value-normalized
   targets. Success metric: permutation invariance > 0.5 before any cost gate. Cost: label set is
   reused (no relabel); ~6–12 GPU-hours for 3 seeds + one eval job, i.e. one cluster day, free.
2. **Training-side fix — fix the labels first.** The edge-argmin rate 0.71 means the grid truncates
   the true optimum in ~7 of 10 states; targets at 40 (and 0) are teaching "order max", which SFT
   then deposits in letter R. Widen the grid (state-relative, or 0..80) and/or lower
   `--p-random`-driven backlog outliers, relabel (~1 CPU-hour), retrain (~half a cluster day).
   Risk: even with clean labels, 0.6B may still only shift priors — the permutation gate should be
   the interim stop-loss, checked on a checkpoint before the full eval.
3. **Accept and move on.** Record wave 7 as: interface positive (rogue tail removed, clean control
   arm), training null at 0.6B. Bank the closed-choice readout as the *eval* harness for any
   future policy (it is deterministic, parse-free, and paired), and spend the next wave elsewhere
   (e.g. the 4B rung, or a non-LLM System-1 head as the SPEC's D8 stretch). Cost: zero compute;
   one afternoon of writing.
